# Brief evaluation rubric

Derived from the owner's Digital Twin Agent Scope document — chiefly the Core
Judgement Layer, the nine Chidimma OS principles, and umbrellas 1, 3, 4, 5, 10,
14, 16, 17, 20, 23, 24 and 25. Each criterion cites its source below.

## How to score

- Extract the record first: client, objective, audience, budget, timeline,
  deliverables, constraints, success measures (PRD: extract fields).
- Rate every criterion **1 to 5** and **quote the exact lines of the brief**
  that justify the score. A score without quoted evidence is invalid.
- Missing information is a finding, not a guess: score what is present, list
  what is missing, and let the gaps drive the questions sent back to the client.
- Write in the owner's voice: concise, intelligent, human, senior, non-cheesy,
  commercially grounded.

## Criteria

### 1. Strategic soundness

- Source: Core Judgement Layer — "Is the thinking sound?"; umbrella 1
  (Executive Strategy & Judgement — identify the real issue, challenge weak
  thinking).
- Definition: The brief pursues a real business problem, not a symptom or a
  borrowed idea.
- 1: No problem stated, or the stated problem is a symptom with an imported
  assumption behind it.
- 3: A real problem, but the reasoning connecting it to the ask is thin or
  untested.
- 5: A clearly owned problem whose logic survives the question "why this,
  why now?".
- Evidence rule: quote the passage that states the problem and the ask.

### 2. Commercial viability

- Source: Core Judgement Layer — "Is this commercially sensible?"; umbrella 17
  (Budgeting & Commercial Discipline — spend has a defensible business
  rationale); principle "Commercial viability matters."
- Definition: The ask is proportionate to the stated budget and carries a
  defensible return.
- 1: No budget, or a budget that cannot plausibly deliver the deliverables.
- 3: Budget present but thin; trade-offs and returns unexamined.
- 5: Budget with enough detail to judge trade-offs and a stated or inferable
  return.
- Evidence rule: quote the budget line, or record its absence verbatim.

### 3. Strategic importance and fit

- Source: Core Judgement Layer — "Is it strategically important?"; umbrella 3
  (Masterbrand Management — reputation, equity, business relevance); umbrella
  20 (Events, Sponsorships & Activations — evaluate strategic fit).
- Definition: The brief sits inside the owner's active areas of work and moves
  something that matters.
- 1: Outside every active workstream; activity for its own sake — remember,
  "activity is not impact."
- 3: Adjacent to an active area, but the connection is asserted, not shown.
- 5: Clearly inside an active workstream with context that makes strong work
  possible.
- Evidence rule: name the workstream and quote the connecting passage; where
  the fit is context rather than brief text, say so explicitly.

### 4. Audience definition and relevance

- Source: Core Judgement Layer — "Does anyone care? Is it appropriate for the
  audience?"; umbrella 4 (Marketing Strategy — translate objectives into target
  audiences); umbrella 14 (Customer Proposition — reasons customers should care).
- Definition: The brief says who the work is for, and that audience is real and
  reachable.
- 1: No audience, or "everyone".
- 3: A broad segment named without differentiating detail.
- 5: A named audience with a distinguishing characteristic that changes the
  work.
- Evidence rule: quote the audience description.

### 5. Distinctiveness

- Source: Core Judgement Layer — "Is it distinctive?"; umbrella 3 (brand
  distinctiveness); umbrella 5 (Campaign Development — pressure-test ideas).
- Definition: The brief points at work that could only come from this owner,
  brand or venture — not a category cliché.
- 1: A me-too ask indistinguishable from competitors' activity.
- 3: A familiar territory with some angle worth pressing.
- 5: A territory or angle with clear room to be distinctive and ownable.
- Evidence rule: quote the passage describing the intended idea or territory.

### 6. Executional clarity

- Source: Core Judgement Layer — "Is the execution good enough? What is the
  simplest, strongest version?"; umbrella 23 (Marketing Operations — triage
  incoming requests, reject weak briefs, identify missing information);
  umbrella 24 (Agency & Vendor Management — strong briefs).
- Definition: Deliverables and timeline are concrete enough to execute, and the
  ask is already its simplest strong version.
- 1: Deliverables unstated or unbounded; no dates; padding everywhere.
- 3: Deliverables and dates present but loose; dependencies unexamined.
- 5: Named deliverables with formats, quantities and a feasible timeline.
- Evidence rule: quote the deliverables and timeline.

### 7. Measurability

- Source: principle "Activity is not impact."; umbrella 25 (Reporting — what
  happened, why it matters, what happens next); umbrella 4 (KPIs).
- Definition: The brief states how success will be judged afterwards.
- 1: Nothing stated.
- 3: A metric named but unattached to a target.
- 5: Metric with a target and a measurement moment.
- Evidence rule: quote the success measure.

### 8. Risk and governance

- Source: Core Judgement Layer — "What could go wrong?"; umbrella 16
  (Regulatory & Marketing Governance — CBN and consumer-protection compliance);
  umbrella 10 (Issues & Crisis Communications — severity scoring); principle
  "Protect reputation without becoming timid."
- Definition: The brief's exposure is understood — reputational, regulatory or
  stakeholder — and manageable.
- 1: Obvious exposure the brief ignores (regulated claims, political
  sensitivity, unresearched partnerships).
- 3: Risk acknowledged but unstated consequences for the work.
- 5: Exposure identified with constraints and approvals already factored in.
- Evidence rule: quote the claim, partner or context creating the exposure;
  where the risk arises from context rather than brief text, say so explicitly.

## From scores to decision

- Any criterion scored 1, or three or more criteria at 2: recommend **decline**,
  with fit as the only stated reason.
- Information missing where a criterion cannot be scored: recommend **ask for
  detail**, listing the specific questions — never more than five.
- Otherwise: recommend **pursue** or proceed to assessment, stating the
  strongest thought first.
- Reputational, regulatory or above-threshold financial exposure: **escalate**
  to the owner regardless of the other scores.

## Judgement checks before the score leaves the harness

Is the thinking sound? Is it commercially sensible? Is it strategically
important? Does anyone care? Is it distinctive? Is it appropriate for the
audience? Is the execution good enough? What could go wrong? What is the
simplest, strongest version?
