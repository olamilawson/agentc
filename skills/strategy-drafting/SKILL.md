# Strategy and brief drafting

## Writing standard

- The owner's voice: concise, intelligent, human, senior, non-cheesy,
  commercially grounded.
- Lead with the point. Every section opens with its conclusion, then earns it.
- One idea per paragraph. Short sentences. No filler openers, no slogans, no
  "in today's fast-paced world".
- Recommend; do not survey. Say what to do, why, and what it costs.
- Facts come from the sources or the owner's answers. Never invent a figure,
  a date, a name or a quotation. Where a fact is needed and missing, write
  `[to confirm: what is needed]` rather than a guess.
- Examine the problem through Nigerian market realities, not an imported
  framework applied blindly.
- Plain markdown inside a section: paragraphs and short lists. No headings
  inside a section; the outline supplies them.

## Before drafting: is anything required missing?

Ask a question only when the answer is required for the document type, cannot
be found in the sources, and would change the document. At most five, each
answerable in a line. If nothing required is missing, ask nothing.

## Templates

An outline follows the template for its document type, one line per section,
and drops a section only when the goal makes it pointless.

### strategy

Required: the business objective, the audience, the budget or its range, the
timeframe.

1. The problem: what is really at stake, in commercial terms.
2. What we know: the evidence, and what it does not tell us.
3. The audience: who matters most and what they need to believe.
4. The strategy: the single choice being made, and what it rules out.
5. How it lives: channels, moments and the role of each.
6. What it costs and returns: budget, trade-offs, the commercial case.
7. Measures: what success looks like and when it is read.
8. Risks: what could go wrong and how it is contained.
9. Next steps: decisions needed, owners and dates.

### brief

Required: the objective, the audience, the deliverables, the budget, the
timeline.

1. Background: why this work, why now.
2. Objective: the one thing the work must do.
3. Audience: who it is for and what is true of them.
4. The single message: what they should take away.
5. Deliverables: formats and quantities.
6. Mandatories and constraints: brand, legal and regulatory.
7. Budget and timeline.
8. How the work will be judged.

### memo

Required: the decision being asked for, the amount, who decides.

1. The recommendation: what is being asked, in one paragraph.
2. Why: the commercial rationale.
3. Options considered: and why the others lose.
4. Cost and return.
5. Risks and how they are contained.
6. What is needed and by when.

## Critique

Check each section against: the stated goal; the evidence available in the
sources and the owner's answers; this writing standard; and the template for
the document type. A fault names the section and says what is wrong in one
line. Faults worth listing: a claim the sources do not support, a section that
does not do its job in the outline, a recommendation hedged into nothing,
padding, cliché, and repetition of another section.
